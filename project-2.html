The Village at Glencliff

Overview

How do you turn one nonprofit's website into a connected campus for donors, hospitals, and neighbors?

The Village at Glencliff (TVG) provides medical respite care and transitional housing for medically vulnerable Nashvillians recovering after hospitalization, illness, or housing loss — with a goal that no one should have to recover from a preventable illness on the street. Every resident who completes the program has remained housed afterward, but TVG's existing website didn't reflect that impact, its updated branding, or its place in Nashville's healthcare continuum.

I joined through the Develop For Good summer program, working alongside two other designers, three engineers, a product manager, and a design/technical manager to redesign TVG's site as a "Connected Campus" — a hub meant to unify storytelling, fundraising, volunteer coordination, and, eventually, a HIPAA-compliant referral portal for hospital discharge planners.

Clinical referral pathway

Laid the UX groundwork for a future HIPAA-compliant portal so hospital discharge planners and social workers can submit documentation and check bed availability.

Ethical storytelling hub

Designed space for mini-documentaries, impact stories, and press coverage, so that their platform says fresh and relevant.

Donor & volunteer engagement

Built donor and volunteer touchpoints tied to real outcomes, supporting TVG's mission of connecting and the goal of increasing awareness of their vision.

Problem

TVG's own website couldn't keep pace with what it had built.

TVG's existing site didn't fully communicate its mission, its updated branding, or its role in the medical respite care lifecycle — and it gave hospital partners, donors, and volunteers no clear next step. Internally, the organization also had no sustainable way for non-technical staff to keep the site updated once a project team moved on, which meant every past improvement risked going stale.

TVG was clear about what mattered most: strong storytelling, visual and social media integration, accessible modern design, and preserving the integrity of a program where every graduate has remained housed since it opened.

Research Process

Five audiences, one site — without diluting any of them.

We mapped separate user flows for donors, corporate sponsors, volunteers, general community members, TVG's own admin staff, and hospital case managers evaluating referrals — each with a different relationship to TVG and a different reason to land on the site. Sites like IJM.org served as a benchmark for how mission-driven organizations balance emotional storytelling with credibility and clear calls to action.

From there, we built out low-fidelity wireframes in Figma across all ten core pages — Home, About Us, Impact Stories, Donate, Volunteer, Referral, Contact, Sustainability, News & Media, and a News & Media blog template — before layering in TVG's brand system: deep blue and green as primary colors, turquoise and gold as accents, paired with Gravita GEO for headers and Freight Sans for body copy.

Low-fidelity wireframes — Events Schedule & About Us

Outcome

Projected to help TVG reach new donors, increase awareness, and stay updated and relevant.

We delivered a complete design system — color, typography, components, and page templates — along with full Figma prototypes for all ten pages, plus a project handoff document so TVG's non-technical staff could keep the site current after our team rolled off. The site is designed to eventually support more hospital referrals, donors, and volunteers each year, with an information architecture built to expand into dedicated referral, donor, and volunteer portals down the line.

Hosting and domain setup were out of scope for our engagement, so the Figma prototype and Webflow handoff — not a live deployment — were our final deliverables to TVG's team.

Reflection

What I learned

Designing inside real constraints

Balancing warm, story-driven UX with the eventual reality of protected health information taught me how much groundwork goes into a healthcare-adjacent product before a single high-fidelity screen gets drawn.

Designing for a team, not just a screen

Working across eight people across the globe split between design and engineering meant our Figma files had to make sense to people who'd never sat in our critiques — I got a lot more intentional about naming layers, documenting components, and writing rationale directly into the file.
